@@part 5 · Differential equations

@@topic c-separable | Separable equations | Sheet 5 · Q1, Q2
@learn
**A differential equation** has a derivative $y'$ in it. Solving it means finding the function $y$.

$y'$ just means $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ (or $\dfrac{dy}{dt}$).

**Separable** = you can get all the $y$ stuff on one side and all the $x$ stuff on the other:
$$\frac{dy}{dx}=g(x)\,h(y)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\int\frac{1}{h(y)}\,dy=\int g(x)\,dx$$
Then integrate both sides, add **one** constant $C$, and get $y$ on its own.

Very common step:
$$\ln|y|=\text{stuff}+c\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=Ce^{\text{stuff}}$$

**Initial condition** like $y(0)=1$: plug it in at the end to find $C$.
@check Is $y'=x\,y^2$ separable?
@checka Yes: $\dfrac{dy}{y^2}=x\,dx$.

@q Sheet 5 · Q1(a)
Solve $y'=ty$ with $y(0)=1$.
@s Separate
$y$ to the left, $t$ to the right:
$$\frac{dy}{y}=t\,dt$$
@s Integrate both sides
$$\ln|y|=\frac{t^2}{2}+c$$
@s Get $y$ on its own
Raise $e$ to both sides:
$$y=Ce^{t^2/2}$$
@s Use $y(0)=1$
Put $t=0$: $\ 1=Ce^0=C$.
@a $y=e^{t^2/2}$

@q Sheet 5 · Q1(b)
Solve $y'=1-y$ with $y(0)=0$.
@s Separate
$$\frac{dy}{1-y}=dt$$
@s Integrate both sides
$\int\frac{dy}{1-y}=-\ln|1-y|$ (the minus comes from the $-y$ inside).
$$-\ln|1-y|=t+c$$
@s Get $y$ on its own
$$\ln|1-y|=-t-c\ \Rightarrow\ 1-y=Ce^{-t}\ \Rightarrow\ y=1-Ce^{-t}$$
@s Use $y(0)=0$
$0=1-C$, so $C=1$.
@a $y=1-e^{-t}$

@q Sheet 5 · Q2(a)
Solve $y'=(1+x)(1+y)$.
@s Separate
$$\frac{dy}{1+y}=(1+x)\,dx$$
@s Integrate both sides
$$\ln|1+y|=x+\frac{x^2}{2}+c$$
@s Get $y$ on its own
$$1+y=Ce^{x+x^2/2}$$
@a $y=Ce^{x+x^2/2}-1$


@@topic c-linear | Linear first order (integrating factor) | Sheet 5 · Q2
@learn
**Linear first order** equations look like:
$$y'+p(x)\,y=q(x)$$
Get it into exactly this shape first ($y'$ with nothing in front of it).

They are usually **not** separable, so we use a trick.

**Integrating factor:**
$$\mu=e^{\int p\,dx}$$
Multiply the whole equation by $\mu$. The left side then becomes a single derivative:
$$(\mu\,y)'=\mu\,q$$
Integrate both sides, then divide by $\mu$.

Handy fact: $e^{\ln A}=A$, so for example $e^{2\ln x}=x^2$.
@check What is $\mu$ for $y'+3y=x$?
@checka $p=3$, so $\mu=e^{\int3\,dx}=e^{3x}$.

@q Sheet 5 · Q2(d)
Solve $\dfrac{dy}{dx}+2xy=xe^{-x^2}$.
@s Match the shape
$y'+p\,y=q$ with $p=2x$ and $q=xe^{-x^2}$.
@s Integrating factor
$$\mu=e^{\int2x\,dx}=e^{x^2}$$
@s Multiply through
The left side becomes $(e^{x^2}y)'$. The right side: $e^{x^2}\cdot xe^{-x^2}=x$.
$$\left(e^{x^2}y\right)'=x$$
@s Integrate
$$e^{x^2}y=\frac{x^2}{2}+C$$
@s Divide by $\mu$
$$y=\left(\frac{x^2}{2}+C\right)e^{-x^2}$$
@a $y=\left(\dfrac{x^2}{2}+C\right)e^{-x^2}$

@q Sheet 5 · Q2(e)
Solve $y'+\dfrac{2y}{x}=\dfrac1{x^2}$.
@s Match the shape
$p=\dfrac2x$, $\ q=\dfrac1{x^2}$.
@s Integrating factor
$$\mu=e^{\int\frac2x\,dx}=e^{2\ln x}=x^2$$
@s Multiply through
$$\left(x^2y\right)'=x^2\cdot\frac1{x^2}=1$$
@s Integrate
$$x^2y=x+C$$
@s Divide by $x^2$
$$y=\frac{x+C}{x^2}$$
@a $y=\dfrac1x+\dfrac{C}{x^2}$


@@topic c-homog | Second order: right side = 0 | Sheet 5 · Q3, Q5
@learn
**The type:**
$$a\,y''+b\,y'+c\,y=0\qquad(a,b,c\text{ are numbers})$$

**Trick:** guess $y=e^{rx}$. Then $y'=re^{rx}$ and $y''=r^2e^{rx}$. Put them in and cancel $e^{rx}$. You get the **characteristic equation**:
$$a\,r^2+b\,r+c=0$$
In practice: swap $y''\to r^2$, $\ y'\to r$, $\ y\to1$. Solve the quadratic.

**Three cases:**

Two different roots $r_1,r_2$:
$$y=C_1e^{r_1x}+C_2e^{r_2x}$$
Same root twice:
$$y=(C_1+C_2x)\,e^{rx}$$
Complex roots $\alpha\pm\beta i$:
$$y=e^{\alpha x}\left(C_1\cos\beta x+C_2\sin\beta x\right)$$

Two constants, because it is second order. Two initial conditions will find them.
@check Characteristic equation of $y''-4y'+3y=0$, and its roots?
@checka $r^2-4r+3=0$, so $(r-1)(r-3)=0$, $r=1,\ 3$.

@q Sheet 5 · Q3(a)
Find the general solution of $y''+3y'+2y=0$.
@s Characteristic equation
$$r^2+3r+2=0$$
@s Solve
$$(r+1)(r+2)=0\ \Rightarrow\ r=-1,\ -2$$
@s Which case?
Two different real roots.
@a $y=C_1e^{-x}+C_2e^{-2x}$

@q Sheet 5 · Q3(c)
Find the general solution of $y''+10y'+25y=0$.
@s Characteristic equation
$$r^2+10r+25=0$$
@s Solve
$$(r+5)^2=0\ \Rightarrow\ r=-5\ \text{(twice)}$$
@s Which case?
Repeated root, so the second part gets an extra $x$.
@a $y=(C_1+C_2x)\,e^{-5x}$

@q Sheet 5 · Q3(d)
Find the general solution of $y''+2y'+2y=0$.
@s Characteristic equation
$$r^2+2r+2=0$$
@s Solve with the quadratic formula
$$r=\frac{-2\pm\sqrt{4-8}}{2}=\frac{-2\pm\sqrt{-4}}{2}=\frac{-2\pm2i}{2}=-1\pm i$$
@s Which case?
Complex: $\alpha=-1$ (real part), $\beta=1$ (the number with $i$).
@a $y=e^{-x}\left(C_1\cos x+C_2\sin x\right)$

@q Sheet 5 · Q5(a)
Solve $y''-16y=0$ with $y(0)=1$, $y'(0)=20$.
@s Characteristic equation
$$r^2-16=0\ \Rightarrow\ r=4,\ -4$$
$$y=C_1e^{4x}+C_2e^{-4x}$$
@s We also need $y'$
$$y'=4C_1e^{4x}-4C_2e^{-4x}$$
@s Use the conditions at $x=0$
$e^0=1$, so:
$$y(0)=C_1+C_2=1$$
$$y'(0)=4C_1-4C_2=20\ \Rightarrow\ C_1-C_2=5$$
@s Solve
Add the two: $2C_1=6$, so $C_1=3$. Then $C_2=1-3=-2$.
@a $y=3e^{4x}-2e^{-4x}$


@@topic c-undet | Second order: right side ≠ 0 | Sheet 5 · Q4, Q5
@learn
Now the right side is **not** zero:
$$a\,y''+b\,y'+c\,y=g(x)$$

**The answer has two parts:**
$$y=y_h+y_p$$
$y_h$ = the solution with right side $0$ (the last topic).

$y_p$ = any one solution that produces $g(x)$. Find it by **guessing its shape**:

$g$ is a polynomial like $3x^2$ → guess $Ax^2+Bx+C$ (all powers up to the top one)

$g=e^{kx}$ → guess $Ae^{kx}$

$g=\sin kx$ or $\cos kx$ → guess $A\cos kx+B\sin kx$

$g$ is a sum → guess the sum of the guesses

Put the guess into the equation and match coefficients to find $A,B,\dots$

**Clash rule:** if your guess already appears in $y_h$, multiply it by $x$.

**Initial conditions go in at the very end**, using the full $y=y_h+y_p$.
@check $y''-y=e^{x}$ has $y_h=C_1e^x+C_2e^{-x}$. What should you guess for $y_p$?
@checka $Ae^x$ is already in $y_h$ (clash), so guess $Axe^{x}$.

@q Sheet 5 · Q4(a)
Find the general solution of $y''-y=3x^2$.
@s Step 1: $y_h$
$r^2-1=0\Rightarrow r=\pm1$.
$$y_h=C_1e^{x}+C_2e^{-x}$$
@s Step 2: guess $y_p$
Right side is a degree 2 polynomial, so:
$$y_p=Ax^2+Bx+C\qquad y_p'=2Ax+B\qquad y_p''=2A$$
@s Put it in
$$2A-(Ax^2+Bx+C)=3x^2$$
@s Match coefficients
$x^2$: $\ -A=3\Rightarrow A=-3$

$x$: $\ -B=0\Rightarrow B=0$

number: $\ 2A-C=0\Rightarrow C=2A=-6$
$$y_p=-3x^2-6$$
@s Add them
$$y=y_h+y_p$$
@a $y=C_1e^{x}+C_2e^{-x}-3x^2-6$

@q Sheet 5 · Q4(c)
Find the general solution of $y''-y'-2y=e^x+x$.
@s Step 1: $y_h$
$r^2-r-2=(r-2)(r+1)=0\Rightarrow r=2,\ -1$.
$$y_h=C_1e^{2x}+C_2e^{-x}$$
@s Step 2: guess $y_p$
Right side is $e^x$ plus a degree 1 polynomial:
$$y_p=De^x+Ax+B$$
No clash: $e^x$ is not in $y_h$.
@s The $e^x$ part
$De^x$ differentiates to itself, so put in $y=y'=y''=De^x$:
$$De^x-De^x-2De^x=-2De^x=e^x\ \Rightarrow\ D=-\tfrac12$$
@s The polynomial part
$y=Ax+B$, $\ y'=A$, $\ y''=0$:
$$0-A-2(Ax+B)=x$$
$x$: $\ -2A=1\Rightarrow A=-\tfrac12$

number: $\ -A-2B=0\Rightarrow B=-\tfrac A2=\tfrac14$
@s Add everything
$$y=y_h-\tfrac12e^x-\tfrac12x+\tfrac14$$
@a $y=C_1e^{2x}+C_2e^{-x}-\dfrac{e^x}{2}-\dfrac x2+\dfrac14$

@q Sheet 5 · Q5(b)
Solve $y''-y'-2y=3e^{2x}$ with $y(0)=0$, $y'(0)=-2$.
@s Step 1: $y_h$
Same left side as the last question:
$$y_h=C_1e^{2x}+C_2e^{-x}$$
@s Step 2: guess $y_p$ (clash!)
Normal guess would be $Ae^{2x}$, but $e^{2x}$ is already in $y_h$. Multiply by $x$:
$$y_p=Axe^{2x}$$
@s Derivatives (product rule)
$$y_p'=A(1+2x)e^{2x}\qquad y_p''=A(4+4x)e^{2x}$$
@s Put it in and cancel $e^{2x}$
$$A(4+4x)-A(1+2x)-2Ax=3$$
The $x$ terms cancel: $4A-A=3A=3$, so $A=1$.
@s Full solution
$$y=C_1e^{2x}+C_2e^{-x}+xe^{2x}$$
$$y'=2C_1e^{2x}-C_2e^{-x}+(1+2x)e^{2x}$$
@s Use the conditions
$y(0)=0$: $\ C_1+C_2=0$

$y'(0)=-2$: $\ 2C_1-C_2+1=-2$

From the first, $C_2=-C_1$. Then $3C_1+1=-2$, so $C_1=-1$, $C_2=1$.
@a $y=-e^{2x}+e^{-x}+xe^{2x}$
