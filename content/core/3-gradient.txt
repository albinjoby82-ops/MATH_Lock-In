@@part 3 · Gradient and tangent planes

@@topic c-gradient | Gradient and directional derivative | Sheet 3 · Q1 to Q3
@learn
**The gradient** $\nabla f$ (say "grad f") is just the partial derivatives put into a vector:
$$\nabla f=\langle f_x,\ f_y\rangle\qquad\text{or in 3D}\qquad\langle f_x,\ f_y,\ f_z\rangle$$

**What it means:** stand on a hill. $\nabla f$ is an arrow pointing **straight uphill**, the steepest way up. Its length tells you how steep.
{{fig:gradient}}
**Directional derivative** $D_{\mathbf u}f$ = the slope if you walk in a direction $\mathbf u$ that you choose.
$$D_{\mathbf u}f=\nabla f\cdot\mathbf u\qquad(\mathbf u\text{ must have length }1)$$

Two tools you need:

**Length:** $\|\langle a,b\rangle\|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$. To make length 1, divide by the length.

**Dot product:** $\langle a,b\rangle\cdot\langle c,d\rangle=ac+bd$.

**Fastest increase:** walk along $\nabla f$. Rate $=\|\nabla f\|$.

**Fastest decrease:** walk the opposite way. Rate $=-\|\nabla f\|$.
@check Make $\langle 3,4\rangle$ into a unit vector.
@checka Length $=\sqrt{9+16}=5$. Divide: $\left\langle\tfrac35,\tfrac45\right\rangle$.

@q Sheet 3 · Q1(b)
Find the gradient of $f(x,y)=\ln(x^2+y^2)$ at $(1,1)$.
@s Partial derivatives
Use $(\ln\text{inside})'=\dfrac{(\text{inside})'}{\text{inside}}$:
$$f_x=\frac{2x}{x^2+y^2}\qquad f_y=\frac{2y}{x^2+y^2}$$
@s Plug in $(1,1)$
The bottom is $1+1=2$.
$$f_x=\frac22=1\qquad f_y=\frac22=1$$
@a $\nabla f(1,1)=\langle1,\ 1\rangle$

@q Sheet 3 · Q2(a)
Find the directional derivative of $f(x,y)=2xy-3y^2$ at $P_0=(5,5)$ in the direction of $\mathbf v=\langle4,3\rangle$.
@s Make the direction a unit vector
Length of $\mathbf v$: $\sqrt{16+9}=5$.
$$\mathbf u=\left\langle\tfrac45,\ \tfrac35\right\rangle$$
@s Gradient
$$f_x=2y\qquad f_y=2x-6y$$
At $(5,5)$:
$$\nabla f=\langle10,\ 10-30\rangle=\langle10,\ -20\rangle$$
@s Dot product
$$D_{\mathbf u}f=10\cdot\tfrac45+(-20)\cdot\tfrac35=8-12=-4$$
Negative means walking that way takes you downhill.
@a $-4$

@q Sheet 3 · Q2(c)
Find the directional derivative of $f(x,y,z)=xy+yz+zx$ at $P_0=(1,-1,2)$ in the direction of $\mathbf v=\langle3,6,-2\rangle$.
@s Unit vector
Length: $\sqrt{9+36+4}=\sqrt{49}=7$.
$$\mathbf u=\left\langle\tfrac37,\ \tfrac67,\ -\tfrac27\right\rangle$$
@s Gradient
$xy+yz+zx$: each partial picks out the terms with that letter.
$$\nabla f=\langle y+z,\ x+z,\ x+y\rangle$$
At $(1,-1,2)$:
$$\nabla f=\langle1,\ 3,\ 0\rangle$$
@s Dot product
$$D_{\mathbf u}f=1\cdot\tfrac37+3\cdot\tfrac67+0=\tfrac37+\tfrac{18}7=\tfrac{21}7=3$$
@a $3$

@q Sheet 3 · Q3(a)
For $f(x,y)=x^2+xy+y^2$ at $P_0=(-1,1)$, find the directions in which $f$ increases and decreases fastest, and the rates in those directions.
@s Gradient
$$\nabla f=\langle2x+y,\ x+2y\rangle$$
At $(-1,1)$:
$$\nabla f=\langle-2+1,\ -1+2\rangle=\langle-1,\ 1\rangle$$
@s Fastest increase = direction of the gradient
Length: $\sqrt{1+1}=\sqrt2$. Unit vector:
$$\mathbf u=\left\langle-\tfrac1{\sqrt2},\ \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\right\rangle$$
@s Fastest decrease = exactly the opposite way
$$-\mathbf u=\left\langle\tfrac1{\sqrt2},\ -\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\right\rangle$$
@s The rates
Fastest up rate = length of gradient $=\sqrt2$. Fastest down $=-\sqrt2$.
@a Increase fastest along $\left\langle-\tfrac1{\sqrt2},\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\right\rangle$ (rate $\sqrt2$); decrease fastest along $\left\langle\tfrac1{\sqrt2},-\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\right\rangle$ (rate $-\sqrt2$).


@@topic c-tangent | Tangent lines and planes | Sheet 3 · Q9, Q10
@learn
**Tangent plane** = the flat plane that just touches a surface at one point (like a sheet of paper resting on a ball).

Write the surface as $F(x,y,z)=\text{constant}$.

**Key fact:** $\nabla F$ points straight out of the surface, at right angles to it. That makes it the "normal" vector of the tangent plane.

A plane through $(a,b,c)$ with normal $\langle A,B,C\rangle$ is
$$A(x-a)+B(y-b)+C(z-c)=0$$

**Recipe:** find $\nabla F$, plug in the point to get $A,B,C$, then use the formula.

For a **curve** in 2D it's the same idea with a line:
$$A(x-a)+B(y-b)=0$$
@check Normal $\langle1,2,3\rangle$, point $(0,0,0)$. Write the plane.
@checka $1(x-0)+2(y-0)+3(z-0)=0$, i.e. $x+2y+3z=0$.

@q Sheet 3 · Q9(a)
Find the tangent line to $x^2+y^2=4$ at $(\sqrt2,\sqrt2)$.
@s Name $F$
$F=x^2+y^2$, and the curve is $F=4$.
@s Gradient at the point
$$\nabla F=\langle2x,\ 2y\rangle=\langle2\sqrt2,\ 2\sqrt2\rangle$$
@s Line formula
$$2\sqrt2(x-\sqrt2)+2\sqrt2(y-\sqrt2)=0$$
@s Tidy up
Divide by $2$: $\ \sqrt2(x-\sqrt2)+\sqrt2(y-\sqrt2)=0$.

Expand: $\sqrt2x-2+\sqrt2y-2=0$.
@a $\sqrt2\,x+\sqrt2\,y=4$

@q Sheet 3 · Q10(a)
Find the tangent plane to $x^2+y^2-z^2=18$ at $(3,5,-4)$.
@s Name $F$
$F=x^2+y^2-z^2$.
@s Gradient at the point
$$\nabla F=\langle2x,\ 2y,\ -2z\rangle=\langle6,\ 10,\ 8\rangle$$
(Careful: $-2z=-2(-4)=+8$.)
@s Plane formula
$$6(x-3)+10(y-5)+8(z+4)=0$$
@s Tidy up
$6x-18+10y-50+8z+32=0\ \Rightarrow\ 6x+10y+8z=36$. Divide by $2$.
@a $3x+5y+4z=18$

@q Sheet 3 · Q10(c)
Find the tangent plane to $\cos(\pi x)-x^2y+e^{xz}+yz=4$ at $(0,1,2)$.
@s Partial derivatives
$$F_x=-\pi\sin(\pi x)-2xy+z\,e^{xz}$$
$$F_y=-x^2+z\qquad F_z=x\,e^{xz}+y$$
@s Plug in $(0,1,2)$
$\sin0=0$ and $e^0=1$:
$$F_x=0-0+2=2\qquad F_y=0+2=2\qquad F_z=0+1=1$$
@s Plane formula
$$2(x-0)+2(y-1)+1(z-2)=0$$
@a $2x+2y+z=4$
