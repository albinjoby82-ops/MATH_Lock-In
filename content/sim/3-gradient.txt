@sim c-gradient-0
Find the gradient of $f(x,y)=\ln(2x+y^2)$ at $(1,2)$.
@s Partial derivatives
Use $(\ln\text{inside})'=\dfrac{(\text{inside})'}{\text{inside}}$:
$$f_x=\frac{2}{2x+y^2}\qquad f_y=\frac{2y}{2x+y^2}$$
@s Plug in $(1,2)$
The bottom is $2+4=6$.
$$f_x=\frac26=\frac13\qquad f_y=\frac46=\frac23$$
@a $\nabla f(1,2)=\left\langle\tfrac13,\ \tfrac23\right\rangle$

@sim c-gradient-1
Find the directional derivative of $f(x,y)=x^2-3xy$ at $P_0=(2,1)$ in the direction of $\mathbf v=\langle3,-4\rangle$.
@s Make the direction a unit vector
Length of $\mathbf v$: $\sqrt{9+16}=5$.
$$\mathbf u=\left\langle\tfrac35,\ -\tfrac45\right\rangle$$
@s Gradient
$$f_x=2x-3y\qquad f_y=-3x$$
At $(2,1)$:
$$\nabla f=\langle4-3,\ -6\rangle=\langle1,\ -6\rangle$$
@s Dot product
$$D_{\mathbf u}f=1\cdot\tfrac35+(-6)\cdot\left(-\tfrac45\right)=\tfrac35+\tfrac{24}5=\tfrac{27}5$$
@a $\dfrac{27}{5}$

@sim c-gradient-2
Find the directional derivative of $f(x,y,z)=x^2+yz$ at $P_0=(1,2,3)$ in the direction of $\mathbf v=\langle2,-1,2\rangle$.
@s Unit vector
Length: $\sqrt{4+1+4}=\sqrt9=3$.
$$\mathbf u=\left\langle\tfrac23,\ -\tfrac13,\ \tfrac23\right\rangle$$
@s Gradient
$$\nabla f=\langle2x,\ z,\ y\rangle$$
At $(1,2,3)$:
$$\nabla f=\langle2,\ 3,\ 2\rangle$$
@s Dot product
$$D_{\mathbf u}f=2\cdot\tfrac23+3\cdot\left(-\tfrac13\right)+2\cdot\tfrac23=\tfrac43-1+\tfrac43=\tfrac53$$
@a $\dfrac53$

@sim c-gradient-3
For $f(x,y)=x^3+y^2$ at $P_0=(1,2)$, find the directions in which $f$ increases and decreases fastest, and the rates in those directions.
@s Gradient
$$\nabla f=\langle3x^2,\ 2y\rangle$$
At $(1,2)$:
$$\nabla f=\langle3,\ 4\rangle$$
@s Fastest increase = direction of the gradient
Length: $\sqrt{9+16}=5$. Unit vector:
$$\mathbf u=\left\langle\tfrac35,\ \tfrac45\right\rangle$$
@s Fastest decrease = exactly the opposite way
$$-\mathbf u=\left\langle-\tfrac35,\ -\tfrac45\right\rangle$$
@s The rates
Fastest up rate = length of gradient $=5$. Fastest down $=-5$.
@a Increase fastest along $\left\langle\tfrac35,\tfrac45\right\rangle$ (rate $5$); decrease fastest along $\left\langle-\tfrac35,-\tfrac45\right\rangle$ (rate $-5$).

@sim c-tangent-0
Find the tangent line to $x^2+y^2=25$ at $(3,4)$.
@s Name $F$
$F=x^2+y^2$, and the curve is $F=25$.
@s Gradient at the point
$$\nabla F=\langle2x,\ 2y\rangle=\langle6,\ 8\rangle$$
@s Line formula
$$6(x-3)+8(y-4)=0$$
@s Tidy up
$6x+8y=18+32=50$. Divide by $2$.
@a $3x+4y=25$

@sim c-tangent-1
Find the tangent plane to $x^2-y^2+z^2=6$ at $(1,2,3)$.
@s Name $F$
$F=x^2-y^2+z^2$.
@s Gradient at the point
$$\nabla F=\langle2x,\ -2y,\ 2z\rangle=\langle2,\ -4,\ 6\rangle$$
@s Plane formula
$$2(x-1)-4(y-2)+6(z-3)=0$$
@s Tidy up
$2x-4y+6z=2-8+18=12$. Divide by $2$.
@a $x-2y+3z=6$

@sim c-tangent-2
Find the tangent plane to $\cos(\pi y)+xy^2+e^{yz}+xz=4$ at $(2,0,1)$.
@s Partial derivatives
$$F_x=y^2+z$$
$$F_y=-\pi\sin(\pi y)+2xy+z\,e^{yz}\qquad F_z=y\,e^{yz}+x$$
@s Plug in $(2,0,1)$
$\sin0=0$ and $e^0=1$:
$$F_x=0+1=1\qquad F_y=0+0+1=1\qquad F_z=0+2=2$$
@s Plane formula
$$1(x-2)+1(y-0)+2(z-1)=0$$
@a $x+y+2z=4$
